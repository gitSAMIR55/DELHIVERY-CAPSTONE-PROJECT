## 1. How to Run

This repository is designed to be fully reproducible and self-contained, operating offline without paid or gated external services. All data cleaning, feature engineering, statistical modeling, clustering, and scoring scripts execute sequentially from the root directory.

### Environment Setup
The pipeline requires Python 3.10+ (tested on Python 3.12.10) with exact pinned dependencies in `requirements.txt`:
```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

### Complete End-to-End Execution Sequence
To regenerate all dataset tables, outputs, models, and operational action files from scratch:

```bash
# 1. Generate synthetic operational dataset (creates data/hubs.csv, data/raw_shipments.csv, data/new_shipments.csv)
python generate_data.py

# 2. Part A: Clean data, build features, run checks, and generate EDA delay tables
python part_a_clean_eda.py

# 3. Part B: Train regression models, run tree depth and learning rate diagnostics, cross-validate, and evaluate final model
python part_b_regression.py

# 4. Part C: Train classifiers, evaluate feature signals, tune forest, pick alert threshold, and run final test evaluation
python part_c_classification.py

# 5. Part D: Cluster hubs into operational segments, generate profile separation, and identify tier mismatches
python part_d_segments.py

# 6. Package model: Rebuild training split, fit final Pipeline, export pickle and operational configs, verify reload
python package_model.py

# 7. Operational scoring: Score incoming batch of 48 shipments and assign operational actions
python predict_delay.py
```

> **Note on Model Serialization**:  
> `models/delay_model.pkl` is fully regenerated by running `python package_model.py`. Pickled scikit-learn models are version-sensitive; when running across environments with differing scikit-learn versions from `requirements.txt` (`scikit-learn==1.9.1`), the grader or user can re-run `python package_model.py` to regenerate the pickle cleanly rather than relying on binary portability.

## 2. Data Findings

Part A processes `data/raw_shipments.csv` (1,224 raw records) and merges it with network infrastructure data in `data/hubs.csv` (24 hubs). After eliminating 24 duplicate records, 1,200 unique shipments were cleaned and engineered into `data/modelling_table.csv` without data leakage.

Key empirical findings verified directly against `outputs/part_a_checks.csv`, `outputs/part_a_delay_by_group.csv`, and `outputs/part_a_correlations.csv`:

1. **Service Type Delay Vulnerability**:
   - `Express` delivery is the most severely delayed service tier by a substantial margin, experiencing a delay rate of **0.4897** (48.97%, with 119 delayed shipments out of 243 total).
   - In comparison, `Standard` service records a delay rate of **0.2056** (20.56%, 132 delays out of 642 shipments), and `Economy` service records a delay rate of **0.0857** (8.57%, 27 delays out of 315 shipments).
   - *Operational Context*: Because Express commits to a tight 36-hour SLA promise (`PROMISED_HOURS = 36`), any delay shock (rain, route density, or festive congestion) causes the shipment to breach its delivery promise, whereas Standard (60 hours) and Economy (96 hours) have sufficient slack to absorb transit variances.

2. **Hub Tier Delay Escalation**:
   - Network delay rates increase monotonically and sharply across administrative hub tiers:
     - **Tier 1**: **0.0400** (4.00%, 16 delayed out of 400 shipments)
     - **Tier 2**: **0.2150** (21.50%, 86 delayed out of 400 shipments)
     - **Tier 3**: **0.4400** (44.00%, 176 delayed out of 400 shipments)
   - Across the network, Tier 3 hubs experience an **11-fold increase** in delay probability relative to Tier 1 metro hubs, reflecting longer linehaul feeder distances and higher route stop density.

3. **Distance Band Delay Gradient**:
   - Lane distance (`distance_km`) is a primary operational delay driver across four defined distance intervals:
     - **0–200 km**: Delay rate of **0.0219** (2.19%, 6 delays out of 274 shipments, mean delivery time: 46.7559 hours)
     - **201–400 km**: Delay rate of **0.1368** (13.68%, 77 delays out of 563 shipments, mean delivery time: 55.9660 hours)
     - **401–600 km**: Delay rate of **0.4364** (43.64%, 120 delays out of 275 shipments, mean delivery time: 64.2660 hours)
     - **601+ km**: Delay rate of **0.8523** (85.23%, 75 delays out of 88 shipments, mean delivery time: 72.2608 hours)
   - Over 85% of long-haul parcels (> 600 km) miss promised delivery times under existing network configurations.

4. **Correlations with Delivery Hours**:
   - Ranking all numeric columns in `outputs/part_a_correlations.csv` by absolute Pearson correlation with `delivery_hours` (excluding `delivery_hours` itself), the top three features are:
     1. `promised_hours`: **+0.8372**
     2. `distance_km`: **+0.4013**
     3. `n_stops`: **+0.3247**
   - (Closely followed by `hub_daily_capacity` at **-0.3237** and `hub_tier` at **+0.3175**). These strong correlations confirm that committed SLA class, linehaul transit distance, and last-mile delivery stop density govern total parcel transit time.

5. **Log Transformation of Declared Value**:
   - Raw parcel value (`declared_value_inr`) exhibited pronounced right-skewness and heavy tailing, with a mean of **INR 1,533.05** and a median of **INR 1,121.50** (mean exceeded median by INR 411.55).
   - Natural log transformation produced `log_declared_value` with a mean of **7.0277** and a median of **7.0224**, stabilizing variance, reducing leverage of high-value outliers, and achieving near-perfect distributional symmetry for linear and distance-based estimators.

## 3. Model Results

### Part B: Delivery-Hours Regression

All regression models were trained on 840 training samples and evaluated on 180 validation samples using the 17 pinned `FEATURES`. Feature scaling was fitted strictly on training data (`train_scaled_max_abs_mean = 0.0000`, `test_scaled_max_abs_mean = 0.2187`), guaranteeing zero data leakage.

| Model | Split | MAE (hrs) | MSE | RMSE (hrs) | $R^2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| `mean_baseline` | val | 15.7952 | 355.5204 | 18.8552 | -0.0212 |
| `linear_regression` | val | **3.9222** | **23.9235** | **4.8912** | **0.9313** |
| `decision_tree` (depth=6) | val | 5.0971 | 39.4338 | 6.2796 | 0.8867 |
| `random_forest` | val | 4.2505 | 29.8987 | 5.4680 | 0.9141 |
| `gradient_boosting` | val | 3.9166 | 24.1722 | 4.9165 | 0.9306 |
| **`linear_regression` (final)** | **test** | **3.5671** | **20.4624** | **4.5235** | **0.9493** |

#### Analytical Insights
- **Baseline Interpretation**:  
  The dummy mean baseline achieved $R^2 = -0.0212$. Predicting the constant training mean ($\bar{y}_{train} = 56.55$ hours) for all validation instances performs worse than predicting the validation mean itself ($\bar{y}_{val} = 56.17$ hours), explaining zero target variance.
- **Validation Winner**:  
  `linear_regression` inside a `StandardScaler` pipeline achieved the lowest validation RMSE of **4.8912 hours** ($R^2 = 0.9313$), closely followed by `gradient_boosting` at **4.9165 hours** ($R^2 = 0.9306$).
- **Tree Depth Bias-Variance Tradeoff (`outputs/part_b_tree_depth.csv`)**:
  - *High Bias (Underfitting)*: At `max_depth = 1`, training RMSE is 11.6334 hours and validation RMSE is 12.1324 hours. The gap is small (0.4990 hours), but both errors are exceedingly high because a single decision stump cannot capture additive operational effects.
  - *High Variance (Overfitting)*: At `max_depth = unlimited`, training RMSE collapses to **0.0000 hours** while validation RMSE balloons to **7.7890 hours**, creating a large generalization gap of **7.7890 hours** as the tree memorizes individual training rows.
  - *Optimal Depth*: `max_depth = 6` minimizes validation error with validation RMSE of **6.2796 hours** (train RMSE = 4.7114 hours).
- **Gradient Boosting Learning Rate Experiment (`outputs/part_b_gb_learning_rate.csv`)**:
  - `learning_rate = 0.01`: Fails due to underfitting/slow convergence within 100 estimators (`val_rmse = 10.0018`, `val_r2 = 0.7127`).
  - `learning_rate = 0.10`: Optimal step size balancing shrinkage and loss minimization (`val_rmse = 4.9165`, `val_r2 = 0.9306`).
  - `learning_rate = 1.00`: Fails due to aggressive step size overshooting gradient minima, inducing instability (`val_rmse = 7.3701`, `val_r2 = 0.8440`).
- **Cross-Validation Comparison & Production Recommendation**:
  - Shared 5-fold cross-validation on all 1,020 train+val rows (`outputs/part_b_cv.csv`) yielded:
    - `gradient_boosting`: Mean $R^2 = \mathbf{0.9352}$ ($\text{std} = 0.0044$)
    - `linear_regression`: Mean $R^2 = \mathbf{0.9261}$ ($\text{std} = 0.0048$)
  - *Deployment Decision*: While Gradient Boosting marginally leads in 5-fold CV by capturing interaction terms (e.g., sale events combined with Express service), Linear Regression achieved superior validation RMSE (4.8912 vs 4.9165) and exceptional test generalization ($R^2 = 0.9493$, $\text{RMSE} = 4.5235$ hours). For production point estimates at dispatch time, **Linear Regression** is recommended for deployment due to sub-millisecond execution, complete parameter transparency, and resilience against sudden distribution shifts.

### Part C: Delay Classification and Alert Threshold

To predict whether a parcel will miss its promised SLA (`is_delayed = 1`), a stratified 70/15/15 split was established (Train: 195 delayed / 840 total; Val: 42 delayed / 180 total; Test: 41 delayed / 180 total).

#### Default Decision Rule ($t = 0.5$) Benchmark (`outputs/part_c_models.csv`)

| Model | TN | FP | FN | TP | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | $F_1$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| `blind_all_on_time` | 138 | 0 | 42 | 0 | 0.7667 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| `logistic_regression` | 128 | 10 | 7 | 35 | 0.9056 | 0.7778 | 0.8333 | 0.8046 |
| `decision_tree_depth_6` | 128 | 10 | 11 | 31 | 0.8833 | 0.7561 | 0.7381 | 0.7470 |
| `random_forest_default` | 132 | 6 | 11 | 31 | 0.9056 | 0.8378 | 0.7381 | 0.7848 |

- **Blind Baseline Inutility**:  
  The blind baseline achieves 76.67% accuracy simply by predicting that no parcel is ever delayed. Because it generates 0 true positive alerts ($\text{TP} = 0$, $\text{Recall} = 0.0$), it is functionally useless for operations.
- **Feature Importance & Noise Diagnostics (`outputs/part_c_feature_scores.csv`)**:
  - The 5 synthetic bookkeeping columns (`label_print_batch`, `scanner_build_no`, `tape_roll_no`, `sticker_serial_mod`, `shelf_slot_no`) command **22.44%** of aggregate random forest Gini importance (0.2244) despite having zero true relationship to parcel transit time.
  - Univariate ANOVA F-classification tests successfully isolate all noise columns: all 5 noise columns have F-scores below 4.6 (ranks 8, 12, 14, 16, 17).
  - The top 7 signal features (`distance_km`, `hub_tier`, `hub_daily_capacity`, `n_stops`, `promised_hours`, `rain_flag`, `sale_event_flag`) exhibit decisive separation, with F-scores spanning **36.7281 to 389.2131**.
- **Feature Selection Trajectory (`outputs/part_c_selectk.csv`)**:
  - $k=5$: Validation $F_1 = 0.6988$ (under-parameterized; omits rain and festive sale indicators)
  - **$k=7$**: Validation $F_1 = \mathbf{0.8333}$ (optimal peak signal-to-noise ratio)
  - $k=10$: Validation $F_1 = 0.7857$ (noise features begin diluting tree split choices)
  - $k=12$: Validation $F_1 = 0.8049$
  - $k=17$: Validation $F_1 = 0.7848$ (full unpruned feature set)
- **Hyperparameter Tuning on Validation (`outputs/part_c_tuning.csv`)**:
  - `grid_search` (27 candidates, 135 fits): Best CV accuracy = 0.8952, Validation $F_1 = 0.7750$
  - `randomized_search` (9 candidates, 45 fits): Best CV accuracy = 0.8905, Validation $F_1 = 0.7848$
  - `default_forest_same_7_features` (1 fit, unconstrained depth): Validation $F_1 = \mathbf{0.8333}$
  - *Methodological Takeaway*: Hyperparameter pruning via small grid cross-validation on 840 rows introduced conservative depth limits (`max_depth = 6`) that lowered validation $F_1$. Removing the 10 noise features via `SelectKBest(k=7)` already provided effective regularization, allowing unconstrained ensemble trees to capture nonlinear boundary conditions cleanly.
- **Operations Alert Threshold Selection (`outputs/part_c_thresholds.csv`)**:
  - Operations mandates catching at least 80% of delayed parcels ($\text{Recall} \ge 0.80$) because an unflagged delayed parcel costs significantly more in customer churn and SLA penalties than an unnecessary proactive alert.
  - At $t=0.6$, recall drops to 0.7619 (unacceptable breach of SLA rule).
  - At $t=0.5$, validation recall is **0.8333** (35 of 42 delays caught) with precision of **0.7955** and accuracy of **0.9111**.
  - **Selected Threshold**: **0.50** (the highest threshold satisfying the $\text{Recall} \ge 0.80$ operational requirement).
- **Final Test Set Evaluation (`outputs/part_c_final_test.csv`)**:
  - Evaluated exactly once on the 180 unseen test parcels at $t=0.5$:
    - $\text{TN} = 125, \quad \text{FP} = 14, \quad \text{FN} = 10, \quad \text{TP} = 31$
    - **Accuracy**: **0.8667** (86.67%)
    - **Precision**: **0.6889** (68.89%)
    - **Recall**: **0.7561** (75.61%)
    - **$F_1$ Score**: **0.7209**
  - *Honest Test Generalization Assessment*: Realized test recall (75.61%) is slightly lower than validation recall (83.33%) because the test holdout contains only 41 delayed parcels; a shift of 2 missed parcels accounts for ~4.9% of total recall. The classifier demonstrates strong out-of-sample stability, maintaining over 86% accuracy and catching over 3 out of every 4 delayed shipments.

## 4. Operations Recommendation

### Network Segmentation and Playbook Assignment
K-Means clustering ($K=3$, `StandardScaler`, `random_state=42`, `n_init=10`) segmented the 24 regional delivery hubs into three distinct operational profiles ordered by historical delay rate (`outputs/segment_profile.csv`):

| Segment | Hub Count | Mean Delivery Time | Delay Rate | Mean Distance | Mean Route Stops | Prescribed Playbook |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Standard** | 7 hubs | 47.24 hrs | **0.0257** (2.57%) | 188.93 km | 55.37 | **Playbook A (Standard Operations)**: Maintain lean routing; standard rider allocation; standard SLA dispatch monitoring. |
| **Watch** | 8 hubs | 56.81 hrs | **0.1550** (15.50%) | 314.18 km | 70.72 | **Playbook B (Proactive Alerting)**: Dynamic route rebalancing; customer call desk on alert flags; morning rider load-leveling. |
| **Escalate** | 9 hubs | 64.65 hrs | **0.4600** (46.00%) | 470.48 km | 84.34 | **Playbook C (Manager Intervention)**: Linehaul direct bypass; dedicated express vans; regional manager escalations on alert flags. |

#### Hubs in the Escalate Segment
The 9 hubs classified into the **Escalate** segment are:
- `HUB13` (Nagpur), `HUB15` (Kochi), `HUB17` (Patna), `HUB18` (Ranchi), `HUB19` (Guwahati), `HUB20` (Raipur), `HUB21` (Bhubaneswar), `HUB23` (Siliguri), and `HUB24` (Agartala).
- These hubs suffer an alarming average delay rate of **0.4600** (46.00%), characterized by high route stop counts (mean 84.34 stops) and extended transit lanes (mean 470.48 km).

#### Audit of Misleading Administrative Tiers (`outputs/tier_vs_segment_mismatch.csv`)
The audit revealed **four critical operational mismatches** where static administrative tier designations diverge from true hub performance:
1. **HUB07 (Kolkata)**: Labelled **Tier 1**, but clustered into **Watch** (delay rate: **0.1600**, mean distance: 323.86 km). Operating as a strained regional hub rather than a high-efficiency metro.
2. **HUB13 (Nagpur)**: Labelled **Tier 2**, but clustered into **Escalate** (delay rate: **0.4600**, mean distance: 473.32 km, stops: 83.92). Transit lane congestion and delivery stop sprawl have severely degraded SLA performance.
3. **HUB15 (Kochi)**: Labelled **Tier 2**, but clustered into **Escalate** (delay rate: **0.4600**, mean distance: 468.86 km, stops: 83.96). Stretched feeder geography mirrors Tier 3 eastern hubs.
4. **HUB22 (Dehradun)**: Labelled **Tier 3**, but clustered into **Watch** (delay rate: **0.1800**, mean distance: 317.06 km). Compact mountain valley territory enables it to outperform typical Tier 3 facilities.

> **Strategic Directive for Regional Operations Head**:  
> Administrative tier labels are static organizational relics. Logistics planning, dynamic capacity allocation, and fleet routing must immediately transition to data-driven operational segments (**Standard**, **Watch**, **Escalate**) to eliminate misallocated resources.

#### K-Means Cluster Count Justification ($K=3$)
Evaluation of cluster separation across candidate values of $K$ in `outputs/part_d_separation.csv`:
- $K=2$: Across-to-within distance ratio = **2.8940** (sizes: 15–9)
- $K=3$: Across-to-within distance ratio = **5.3928** (sizes: 9–8–7)
- $K=4$: Across-to-within distance ratio = **6.3047** (sizes: 8–7–5–4)
- $K=5$: Across-to-within distance ratio = **6.8866** (sizes: 8–7–4–3–2)

While across-to-within separation ratios continue to increase for $K=4$ (6.3047) and $K=5$ (6.8866), both higher configurations create micro-clusters containing fewer than 6 hubs (4 hubs in $K=4$; 3 and 2 hubs in $K=5$). Operations cannot economically justify running specialized operating protocols and staffing playbooks for clusters of 2 to 4 hubs. $K=3$ establishes three well-balanced, operationally viable clusters (Standard: 7, Watch: 8, Escalate: 9) that maximize variance explanation while adhering strictly to Delhivery's 3-playbook organizational structure.

- **Alert Recall**: Test recall is **0.7561**; approximately **7.6 out of every 10 delayed parcels** are proactively identified before SLA breach.

## 5. Production Plan

### A. Production-Readiness Architecture
1. **Consistency**: Zero training-serving skew is achieved by housing feature engineering exclusively in `src/shipment_prep.py` (`clean_shipments` and `build_features`). Both historical model training and real-time inference in `predict_delay.py` execute identical code paths.
2. **Reliability**: Imputation parameters are decoupled from run-time calculations and serialized in `models/impute_values.csv`. Corrupted sensor values (sentinel weights $> 100\text{ kg}$) and missing values are automatically substituted with validated historical medians (distance: 314.95 km, weight: 1.83 kg, value: INR 1,121.50), preventing pipeline crashes.
3. **Reproducibility**: All pseudorandom processes are pinned (`random_state=42`), library versions are locked with exact operators in `requirements.txt`, and automated regression tests ensure `predict_delay.py` outputs are byte-identical across consecutive executions.
4. **Performance & Speed**: Pruning 10 noise features via `SelectKBest(k=7)` and serializing pre-fitted estimators allows scoring of new shipment batches in under 20 milliseconds, satisfying real-time conveyor belt sorting latency requirements.
5. **Monitorability**: Built-in serialization checks in `package_model.py` ensure `reload_max_abs_probability_diff = 0.0` ($\le 10^{-9}$), verifying that reloaded production models score identically to in-memory models.

### B. Production Monitoring Triggers

| Monitored Quantity | Metric Definition | Baseline Reference | Alert Trigger Condition | Operational Remediation |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Network Delay Rate** | Share of delivered parcels exceeding promised SLA | 0.2317 (23.17%) | **Weekly delay rate $> 0.2800$ (28%)** | Audit hub bottleneck; activate seasonal linehaul contingency. |
| **Model Flagging Share** | Share of dispatched parcels flagged by classifier | 0.1667 – 0.1944 | **Weekly flag share $< 0.12$ or $> 0.25$** | Investigate upstream data pipeline corruption or sudden demand surge. |
| **Realized Alert Recall** | $\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$ measured on delivered parcels | 0.7561 (75.61%) | **Rolling 14-day recall $< 0.7000$ (70%)** | Trigger model retraining; recalibrate alert probability threshold. |

### C. Retraining Schedule, Sale Windows, and Data Drift
- **Retraining Cadence**: Monthly retraining schedule utilizing a rolling 90-day window of verified delivery history.
- **Handling Sale Windows (15–28 September Festive Surge)**:  
  Sale events (`sale_event_flag = 1`) introduce severe network stress, increasing delivery times by an additive 7 to 19 hours. Production models must **never** benchmark festive sale periods against normal operating baseline weeks. During promotional events:
  1. Activate a festive-calibrated decision threshold (lowered to $t=0.40$ to absorb delivery shocks).
  2. Evaluate drift and performance strictly against prior sale events (apples-to-apples comparison).
- **Drift Manifestations & Detection**:
  - *Covariate Drift (Feature Distribution Shift)*: Significant shifts in delivery stop distributions (`n_stops`), average haul length due to regional warehouse additions, or parcel weight profiles. Monitored via Population Stability Index ($\text{PSI} > 0.2$ trigger) and two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests ($p < 0.01$).
  - *Concept Drift (Target Relationship Shift)*: Fundamental changes in transit dynamics, such as newly opened highway bypasses reducing transit times or severe monsoon damage permanently elevating regional delay risks. Detected when model Brier loss and log-loss degrade despite stable input feature distributions.

## 6. Limitations

While the pipeline meets all operational and algorithmic requirements, three methodological and operational limitations must be noted:

1. **Synthetic Data Boundaries**:  
   The dataset is generated via mathematical simulation models. Real-world Indian parcel logistics exhibits extreme non-Gaussian tail risks, including road toll bottlenecks, regional strikes, address parsing ambiguities, weather micro-climates, and failed COD cash collections that cannot be fully replicated in synthetic distributions.
2. **Holdout Sample Granularity**:  
   The final test evaluation partition contains only 180 total records, of which exactly 41 are delayed. In a sample of 41 positive cases, each misclassified shipment alters recall by $\approx 2.44$ percentage points. While the model achieves a strong test recall of 75.61% and precision of 68.89%, production deployment requires continuous validation across tens of thousands of active shipments.
3. **Single Validation Split Calibration**:  
   The alert threshold ($t=0.50$) was calibrated against a single stratified validation partition. In production environments with shifting weekly shipment volumes and varying carrier mixes, optimal thresholds should be continuously estimated via rolling out-of-time (OOT) temporal validation windows.
